## Design pins

- THE SEAM RULING: derive-walk exclusion, not the `pendingSources` terminal-stamp check. Grounded: a dead row needs no dispatch under ANY outcome — its very FIRST dispatch (a namespace-free dead doc derives its whole chain pending) already fires the unguarded Adopt on a successful settle, which a pendingSources stamp check never prevents; the walks feed both derive and reenrich; one seam (two helper bodies) kills every dead-row dispatch; the stamp check would also CHANGE live-row re-attempt behavior (skipped sources legitimately re-derive per pass today) — out of scope by design.
- Live-row safety: the filter is the exact `deleted` comparison — every live lifecycle status (new/parsed/enriched/ready/failed/stale/...) passes unchanged; `AdoptStranded`'s own candidate walk reads `QueryItems(…, "deleted", …)` directly and is NOT touched by this seam.
- Self-heal interaction: a healed row's status advanced to `ready` by the re-scan upsert (2.2's `nextScanState`) — re-included on the very next walk; adoption re-fires on its settle. Reenrich exclusion is REQUIRED for heal correctness: stripping a tombstoned row's namespace would destroy the carry-forward facts D3 heals with.
- The fire guard uses `row.status` (the dispatch-loaded lifecycle) and the enrich-owned literal — never a scan import, never a fresh store read (the settle already persisted the row; the guard is defense-in-depth behind the seam, cheap by construction).
- The self-move guard is a pure early return — no tx begun, no lock taken, error-free `(0, 0)`; the store docs state the invariant (a move's source and target are always distinct ids; the caller's fire guard + seam make the collision unreachable, the guard makes it harmless).
